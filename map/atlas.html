Where dual-credit math is out of reach

Dual credit is widespread in Indiana, but the course that leads into college mathematics is not. This atlas maps every traditional public high school by whether it reports dual enrollment and whether it offered calculus in either of the last two federal civil-rights collections.

Definitions. Calculus and dual enrollment count as offered if the school reported them in either CRDC 2021-22 or 2023-24; small schools often run calculus in alternate years. "Thin pathway" means no calculus and fewer than one in three graduates (cohorts 2021–2024) earning dual credit from an Indiana public college. Dual-credit share and free/reduced-lunch share come from Indiana Commission for Higher Education graduate breakouts; enrollment is CRDC 2023-24. Virtual and alternative schools are excluded.

Limits. Neither federal nor state files identify the subject of dual-credit courses, so "dual-credit math" is approximated by the calculus pipeline plus overall dual-credit uptake. A school without calculus can still offer dual-credit finite math or college algebra.
