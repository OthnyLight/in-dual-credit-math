## Title
Who Dual Credit Reaches, and Where Math Can't Start: School-Level Evidence from Indiana

## Session description (186 words)
Indiana graduates earn dual credit at high rates (62% of the 2021–2024 public high school cohorts studied), but does a school's dual-credit uptake translate into better first-year college performance, and can every school offer the mathematics that leads into it? Linking Commission for Higher Education outcomes to federal Civil Rights Data Collection course data for 359 public high schools, we find that higher-uptake schools send graduates with modestly higher first-year GPAs and more credits after accounting for poverty, AP success, Algebra I timing, and math offerings; about 80% of the raw advantage reflects which schools offer dual credit, and part of the rest is dual-credit work counted on the college record. Within schools, the gap between dual-credit earners and their classmates widens as programs grow. Meanwhile 20% of traditional public high schools offered no calculus in either recent year, rising to 41% of the highest-poverty schools. Drawing on experience teaching IU Advance College Project MATH M118 (Finite Mathematics) in partner high schools, the session walks through an interactive access atlas and a replicable public-data method that partnerships in any state can run on their own schools.

## Learning outcomes
1. Interpret school-level dual-credit outcome data without mistaking selection for program effect.
2. Identify schools in their own service area where the mathematics pathway into dual credit is missing, using public federal data.
3. Apply a replicable, open method (code and data provided) to evaluate their partnership's readiness outcomes.
